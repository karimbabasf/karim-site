To the Collection Engineering team, Elliptic18 August 2026

Hello,

I have been in crypto since 2021 and I have spent the last two years building agents that other people depend on. Your listing is the first one I have read where those are the same job. The pipeline you describe, ingest, classify, store, review, is the loop I keep rebuilding on my own time. The only thing mine is missing is a consequence as sharp as tracing stolen funds.

What I can prove

  • I already own a collection pipeline. Warden, my macOS app, tails every AI coding session on a machine into a Rust core, classifies it, and lands it in a searchable store that feeds one live view of the whole fleet. When I made an indexing path 13 times faster, I proved it by replaying the old and new code against a copy of the real store and diffing all 4,019 rows, accepting only zero differences as a pass.
  • I ship agents that run unattended, in TypeScript, on Postgres. I built and operate the voice agent that answers the phone for a business I co-run. It resolves billing and order questions against live data, hands to a human the moment it should, and records, transcribes and summarises every call into an operations surface. I rebuilt it twice off real recordings, and the second root cause was not the prompt: the live assistant was quietly running a different model than every comment in the repo claimed.
  • I write software that touches real money and treats that as the point. Phosphor, my own MCP server, exposes 38 tools behind a permission gate, runs on mainnet against real balances, and carries 993 tests. It validates an address by decoding base58 and checking the byte count rather than matching a pattern, because a payment to a mistyped address is settled and gone.
  • The crypto is first-hand, not researched. Since 2021: DeFi from the inside, early on Arbitrum, Optimism and zkSync, active through the cycles. I also run business development in San Francisco for 1Claw, which builds custody and an LLM firewall for AI agents, so my weeks are spent reading protocol architecture and on-chain behaviour rather than decks.

How I work with AI

AI did not make me faster. It changed what I am willing to attempt. Everything above was built by directing agents rather than typing every line, which is also why Warden exists: I needed to see what a fleet of them was actually doing before I could trust the output. I publish my own agent skills as a public MIT repository so the people around me can run the workflow instead of hearing me describe it, and I keep a written record of every architecture decision and every failure, so the next agent starts where the last one stopped. That practice is a year old and it still changes every month.

Why here

I am self-taught and I have chosen every problem I have worked on so far. What I want next is a team that has been doing this longer than I have, on data that matters to somebody outside the room. Elliptic has been at it since 2013, and your output ends up in the hands of the people who actually stop this crime. That is a rare thing to be able to point at.

I applied to Collection Engineering because it is the closest fit I could see from the outside. I care more about doing this work at Elliptic than about which door I come through, so if another team, another level, or a short piece of trial work is the better place to start, I would take that conversation just as seriously. Give me the recurring problem nobody wants to do by hand, and I will automate it away.

Thank you for your time,
